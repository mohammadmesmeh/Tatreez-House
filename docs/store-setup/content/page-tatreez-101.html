Tatreez (تطريز) is the Palestinian art of hand embroidery, worked mostly in cross-stitch on linen or cotton. For generations, women stitched it onto thobes, cushions and wedding pieces, and the motifs, colours and placement on a garment could hint at where its maker came from.

What makes tatreez recognisable

  • Cross-stitch: each motif is built from small X-shaped stitches counted on the weave of the fabric.
  • Geometric motifs: stars, diamonds, cypress trees and borders, repeated and combined into larger panels.
  • Placement: chest panels, sleeve cuffs and long side panels are the traditional places for embroidery on a thobe.

Motifs you'll find in our collection

  • Cypress tree: a tall, stepped tree shape, one of the most widely used motifs.
  • Eight-pointed star: a star built from a square and a diamond, used on its own or in borders.
  • Moon of Bethlehem: an eight-armed star medallion, often stitched as a centrepiece.
